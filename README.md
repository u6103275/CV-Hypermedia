1. PROJECT ANALYSIS
   
   - User Persona: Carla Armengol
     
       Carla Armengol works in the recruitment department of a technology company in Girona. She's bringing opportunities to young              people that want to have the first labor experience at this sector. 

       She wants to quickly understand a candidate's academic background, technical  knowledge, practical experience, and personal              skills. Not only that, but she's also interested in seeing examples of university projects to evaluate the candidate's abilities.

       Besides, Carla searches a website with clear navigation, well-organized information, and an easy-to-read design. And she doesn't         want to spend too much time searching for relevant details.

   - Information architecture:

       The website follows a single-page structure, where all the information is organized vertically into different sections. I             chose this structure because it allows visitors to explore my CV naturally by scrolling, without having to navigate between           multiple pages. It also makes the information easier to find and understand.

       At the top of the page, there is a header containing my name and a navigation menu. The menu includes links to About Me,              Education, Projects, and Contact. These links allow visitors to move directly to specific sections of the website.

       The first section is the Hero section, which introduces me through my name, a short description, and a photograph. It also            contains a button that takes the visitor directly to my projects. I decided to place this information first because it                provides an immediate introduction to who I am.

       The About Me section presents a more personal description of my interests and personality. It is followed by Education, which         contains my academic background, including my current Computer Engineering degree and previous studies.

       In addition, the Skills section is divided into two columns: technical skills and soft skills. This separation makes the              information easier to scan and compare.

       The Experience section describes my professional activities and responsibilities, while Projects presents two university              projects related to databases and object-oriented programming.

       Finally, the Contact section provides my email address and GitHub profile. I placed it at the bottom because visitors may want        to contact me after reviewing my CV.

   - Visual design:

       For the visual design of my CV website, I wanted to create a clean, simple, and professional appearance. My main objective was        to make the information easy to read while maintaining a modern and consistent style. I decided to follow the structure of a          traditional CV, adapting it to a website format.

       The color palette combines dark navy blue, soft gray, white, and purple. Dark navy blue (#1E293B) is used for the main                headings because it provides good contrast and a professional appearance. Gray (#64748B) is used for secondary information and        descriptions. White (#FFFFFF) and a very light gray (#F8F9FC) are used as section backgrounds. Alternating these backgrounds          helps distinguish the different parts of the website without adding unnecessary decorative elements.

       Purple (#6366F1) is the main accent color. I used it for small section labels, decorative lines, dates, icons, and the main           button. This creates visual consistency and highlights important elements.

       For typography, I chose a simple sans-serif style, using Inter with Arial as a fallback. I established a visual hierarchy             through different font sizes and weights, making section titles more prominent than descriptions and secondary information.

       The layout uses generous margins, padding, and spacing between elements. These decisions help prevent the page from feeling           crowded. In sections such as Skills, I used a two-column layout to organize information efficiently.

       I also used CSS Flexbox to align the navigation menu, the introductory text and photograph, and the dates displayed next to           education and experience entries.

       The design was initially created in Figma, where I defined the color variables and organized the different sections. I then           reproduced this design using HTML and CSS, trying to maintain consistency between the prototype and the final website.
